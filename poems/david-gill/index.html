David Gill

He was miserable, David Gill. Tried to teach me poetry, but his wife had just died. I despised him. He wrote about gravestones on the ground, at the church at the University of Huddersfield. He asked why we respect the dead but walk across those stones and pay them no notice. That impressed me some. He said he never knew where to write till he found himself at the kitchen table, suffering a loss he didn't mention. Now I smoke and drink and worry at the kitchen table and have found a voice to write, a friend of misery and poetry again at forty.
